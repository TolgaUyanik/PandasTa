# `ts-energy-last-20` — log

## What was built

tsfresh's `energy_ratio_by_chunks(num_segments=4, segment_focus=3)` over
20 simple returns, as PandasTa issue #102 states it, shipped as
`pandas_ta/statistics/ts_energy_last_20.py`, column
**`TS_ENERGY_LAST_20_4`**.

    r            = close / close.shift(1) - 1
    tail         = length // 4
    TS_ENERGY    = sum(r[-tail:] ** 2) / sum(r ** 2)

The column is the share of a 20-return window's squared-return energy
that sits in its newest 5 bars: 0.25 is an even spread, near 1 every big
move was recent, near 0 they were all old.

Two decisions did the work, and both came out of a measurement rather
than a preference.

1. **The window reduction is `ew.sum(axis=1)` over a
   `sliding_window_view`, not a left-to-right accumulation.** The first
   draft accumulated left to right, by analogy with
   `statistics/ts_imq_20.py`, whose scan has to be sequential because it
   compares a running total against a threshold. Measured, that spelling
   is 3 to 4 ulps from the source's own `np.sum` and is farther from an
   exact rational oracle on 10 of 10 frames. The pairwise reduction is
   **bit-equal** to the source instead, because `sliding_window_view`
   gives each row a stride of one double, so a row is a contiguous run of
   `length` doubles and `axis=1` reduces it exactly as `np.sum` reduces
   the source's `(length,)` array. Gate A is therefore bit-equality and
   not a tolerance.
2. **`length // 4` is the source's arithmetic, not a simplification of
   it.** `np.array_split` hands the LONGER chunks out first, so
   `segment_focus = num_segments - 1` is always the trailing
   `length // num_segments` values. Checked against `np.array_split`
   itself — size and indices — for every length from 4 to 240.

Everything else is evidence. `docs/measure_ts_energy_last_20.py` has
fourteen sections and prints every number in
`docs/TsEnergyLast20Measured.md` and in the test module.
